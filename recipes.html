Seasonal recipes for real weeknights

Six dependable recipes we come back to again and again. Each one is measured in cups and grams, tested at home, and easy to adapt to whatever is in season.

Roasted squash soup with crispy sage

  • Serves 4
  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 45 min
  • Autumn

Roasting the squash before it goes into the pot gives this soup a deep, almost nutty sweetness that boiling alone never manages.

Ingredients

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C). Peel, seed and cube the squash, then toss with the onion, garlic, 2 tbsp oil and the salt.
  2. Roast on a large tray for 35 minutes, until soft and caramelised at the edges.
  3. Tip everything into a pot, add the stock and simmer for 10 minutes. Blend until very smooth, adding a splash of water if it is too thick.
  4. Fry the sage leaves in the remaining oil for 30 seconds until crisp. Serve the soup with a swirl of cream or yogurt and the crispy sage.

Tip: the soup freezes well for up to three months. Leave out the cream until you reheat it.

Crunchy pear salad with toasted seeds

  • Serves 2–4
  • 15 min
  • No cooking
  • Autumn & winter

Crisp, sweet and a little sharp. This is the salad we make when we want something fresh alongside a rich autumn dinner.

Ingredients

Method

  1. Toast the seeds in a dry pan with a drop of oil over medium heat for 3–4 minutes, shaking often, until golden. Tip onto a plate to cool.
  2. Shake the dressing ingredients together in a jar until combined.
  3. Core the pears and slice them thinly. Toss the leaves with most of the dressing.
  4. Arrange the pear slices over the leaves, drizzle with the remaining dressing and scatter with the seeds just before serving.

Tip: slice the pears at the last minute, or toss them in a little lemon juice, so they do not brown.

Simple fresh pasta dough

  • Serves 4
  • Prep 20 min
  • Rest 30 min
  • All year

Fresh pasta sounds intimidating, but it is only flour and eggs. A rolling pin works perfectly if you do not own a pasta machine.

Ingredients

Method

  1. Pile the flour on a clean surface, make a well in the middle and crack in the eggs. Add the salt and oil.
  2. Beat the eggs with a fork, gradually drawing in flour from the edges, until a shaggy dough forms.
  3. Knead for 8–10 minutes until smooth and springy. Wrap and rest for 30 minutes at room temperature.
  4. Roll the dough out very thinly on a floured surface, dust with flour, loosely roll it up and slice into ribbons. Cook in plenty of salted boiling water for 2–3 minutes.

Tip: in autumn, toss the ribbons with brown butter, roasted squash and grated hard cheese.

No-knead country loaf

  • 1 loaf
  • Prep 10 min
  • Rise 12–18 hr
  • Bake 45 min

Time does the kneading for you. Mix it in the evening, bake it the next day, and enjoy a crackly crust with an open, chewy crumb.

Ingredients

Method

  1. Stir the flour, salt and yeast together in a large bowl. Add the water and mix until no dry flour remains. The dough will be sticky.
  2. Cover and leave at room temperature for 12–18 hours, until bubbly and more than doubled.
  3. Turn out onto a floured surface, fold the edges into the middle to form a ball, and rest seam-side down on floured parchment for 1–2 hours.
  4. Heat the oven to 450°F (230°C) with a lidded heavy pot inside for 30 minutes. Lower the dough in on its parchment, cover and bake 30 minutes, then uncover and bake 10–15 minutes more until deep brown.

Tip: let the loaf cool for at least an hour before slicing. Cutting it hot makes the inside gummy.

Small-batch plum jam

  • 2 small jars
  • Prep 15 min
  • Cook 25 min
  • Late summer

A small batch that fits in one pan, with no special equipment. Plums are naturally high in pectin, so the jam sets beautifully.

Ingredients

Method

  1. Put a small plate in the freezer. Combine the plums, sugar and lemon juice in a wide pan and leave for 10 minutes so the juices start to run.
  2. Bring to a boil, stirring until the sugar dissolves, then boil steadily for 15–20 minutes, stirring more often as it thickens.
  3. To test, spoon a little onto the cold plate, wait 1 minute and push it with your finger. If it wrinkles, it is ready.
  4. Pour into clean jars, seal and let cool. Store in the fridge and use within 3 weeks.

Tip: this is a refrigerator jam. For long-term shelf storage, follow tested home-canning guidance for sterilising and processing jars.

Weeknight grain bowl with roasted vegetables

  • Serves 2
  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 30 min
  • All year

A formula more than a recipe: a grain, something roasted, something fresh, something crunchy and a punchy dressing. Change it with the seasons.

Ingredients

Method

  1. Cook your grain according to the packet instructions.
  2. Meanwhile, roast the vegetables and chickpeas with oil and salt at 425°F (220°C) for 25 minutes, until browned.
  3. Whisk the tahini, lemon and garlic, adding water a spoonful at a time until it is pourable. Season with salt.
  4. Divide the grain between bowls, top with the roasted vegetables, herbs and a generous drizzle of dressing.

Tip: cook a double batch of grains at the weekend. They keep in the fridge for 4 days.

Cooking times are a guide, as ovens and stoves vary. Always check ingredient labels if you are cooking for someone with allergies. See our Disclaimer for more.
