Through the year

A cook’s guide to the four seasons

What to buy, how to store it and how to cook it. Use this guide alongside the monthly produce calendar on our homepage.

SpringSummerAutumnWinterShopping tips
Tender, green and quick to cook

Spring

After a long winter of roots and brassicas, spring brings delicate green vegetables that need very little cooking. This is the season of artichokes, asparagus and peas: treat them gently and they taste wonderful.

Vegetables

  • Artichokes
  • Asparagus
  • Peas & fava beans
  • Spinach
  • Radishes
  • Spring onions

Fruit

  • Rhubarb
  • Early strawberries
  • Late citrus

Prep & storage: To prepare an artichoke, snap off the tough outer leaves until you reach pale ones, trim the top third and peel the stem. Rub cut surfaces with lemon so they do not darken, then steam for 25–35 minutes until a leaf pulls away easily.

Ripe, juicy and best left simple

Summer

Summer produce is so full of flavour that the best thing a cook can do is get out of the way. Slice tomatoes with salt and oil, grill corn and zucchini, and eat stone fruit straight from the bag.

Vegetables

  • Tomatoes
  • Sweet corn
  • Zucchini
  • Eggplant
  • Peppers
  • Green beans

Fruit

  • Peaches & nectarines
  • Plums
  • Cherries
  • Berries
  • Melon

Prep & storage: Store tomatoes and stone fruit at room temperature, stem-side down, out of direct sun. Refrigerate them only once they are fully ripe and you need an extra day or two.

Sweet, earthy and made for the oven

Autumn

As the days shorten, produce gets heartier. Squash, apples, pears and root vegetables are built for roasting, braising and baking, and they fill the kitchen with warm, comforting smells.

Vegetables

  • Winter squash & pumpkin
  • Sweet potatoes
  • Brussels sprouts
  • Beets
  • Cauliflower
  • Kale

Fruit

  • Apples
  • Pears
  • Figs
  • Late plums
  • Cranberries
  • Quince

Prep & storage: Whole winter squash keep for one to three months somewhere cool, dry and dark. Once cut, wrap the pieces and refrigerate, using them within five days.

Bright citrus and hardy greens

Winter

Winter cooking leans on storage vegetables and sturdy greens, balanced by the sunshine of citrus fruit. Slow soups and stews shine now, and a little lemon or orange keeps them lively.

Vegetables

  • Kale & cabbage
  • Leeks
  • Parsnips
  • Turnips
  • Celery root
  • Carrots

Fruit

  • Oranges & clementines
  • Grapefruit
  • Lemons
  • Pomegranates
  • Stored apples & pears

Prep & storage: Store root vegetables in the fridge’s crisper drawer with any leafy tops removed; the leaves draw moisture from the roots. Many tops, such as carrot and beet greens, are good to eat too.

All year round

Seven habits for smarter produce shopping

  1. Shop with a loose plan. Decide how many meals you need, not exactly which ingredients.
  2. Look for abundance. Big, colourful displays usually mean peak season and fair prices.
  3. Use your senses. Ripe fruit smells fragrant; fresh greens feel crisp and look bright.
  4. Buy delicate things last. Berries, herbs and leaves go on top of the bag and get eaten first.
  5. Talk to growers. Market stallholders are often happy to explain how they cook what they sell.
  6. Store it properly at home. A few minutes putting things away correctly adds days of freshness.
  7. Plan a clear-out meal. Soups, frittatas and grain bowls happily use up odds and ends at the end of the week.
